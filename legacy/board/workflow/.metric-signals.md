## Thesis in one line

No single field measures real demand. The retail-channel fields (`consumption`,
`unitsSoldAnHour`, retail turnover, `amountSoldRestaurant`) see **only goods with a consumer
identity** and are blind to the entire B2B intermediate layer. The B2B layer's demand exists
**only** in the recipe graph and must be *derived* by propagating real leaf-sink demand up the
tree. The single decisive resolver for crude-vs-aerospace is recipe-graph propagation using
`consumption` as the leaf sink — verified below to reproduce the known 0.057/h aerospace figure.

## Signal inventory (each: what it is, reliability, known-truth scorecard)

### S1. `consumption` field (game-facts) — realm NPC consumer-consumption rate, units/h
- **What it actually is:** the realm's simulated end-consumer draw for a good *in its own right*
  (retail + NPC). NOT industrial input consumption. Proof: crude=0, dough=0, plastic=0,
  carbon-fiber=0, every engine=0 — all obviously consumed industrially, yet read 0. It fires
  only for goods with a consumer/retail identity (apples 12.46, planks 13.41, steak 1.04,
  petrol 19.45, diesel 19.54, samosas 0.227).
- **Reliability as a *direct* demand read: MEDIUM-HIGH for retail goods, ZERO for B2B goods.**
  It is fractional and honest (no placeholder inflation) — this is its key virtue over S2.
- **Scorecard direct:** correctly ranks apples/planks/steak > samosas as consumer goods.
  **FAILS OPEN on crude (0), dough (0), all engines (0)** — would kill them. **This is exactly
  the field the task warns against using alone.** Its real power is as the *leaf sink* for S8.

### S2. `unitsSoldAnHour` field (game-facts) — retail throughput cap, units/h
- **Reliability: MEDIUM, and it is booby-trapped.** For mass retail goods it is a real cap
  (apples 110, planks 120, steak 35, petrol 103, diesel 105 — all consistent with S1/retail).
- **Placeholder-floor trap (verified):** high-tier finals carry a fake **"1"** that they do not
  actually sell: satellite=1 (real consumption 0.0115), jumbojet=1 (0.0027), private-jet=1
  (0.0078), single-engine=1 (0.0188), truck=1 (0.090), luxury-car=0.5 (0.108). Any metric that
  feeds `unitsSoldAnHour` into a recipe multiplier **manufactures mirages** — this is the
  mechanism behind the paper-$/h bug at the aerospace end (a ×34 rocket-engine coefficient on a
  fake "1" satellite sink). **Use `consumption`, not this field, as the propagation leaf.**
- **Scorecard:** = 0 for crude/dough/engines (would kill them, same failure as S1). Reads a
  fake positive for aerospace finals (would *keep* them — wrong direction). Worst single field.

### S3. Retail turnover time-series (market-snapshots.jsonl + board-data `cmo.retail[].retailData`)
- **What it is:** per-retail-good `{saturation, averagePrice, demand}`, snapshot rows plus a
  **28-day daily history** in board-data. `saturation` = retail-lake fill (drain/growth signal),
  `demand` = retail headroom index (≈ 0.95 at sat 0.1, goes negative above sat ~1.8; it is a
  headroom score, **not a volume**), `averagePrice` trend = price pressure.
- **Reliability: HIGH — but only within its coverage window.** This is the one signal that can
  tell a *draining* lake from a *growing* one (turnover), which no static field can.
- **Coverage boundary (critical):** the feed is keyed by `dbLetter` and covers ~80 retail goods
  **only**. It **does not contain crude(10), dough(137), plastic(19), carbon-fiber(75), or any
  engine (52,80–89)** — it is blind to precisely the hard B2B cases. It *does* contain aerospace
  **finals** (95 jumbojet, 96 private-jet, 97 single-engine, 99 satellite) but with **no
  `averagePrice`** and saturation 1.24–1.30 → correctly reads them as dead/thin.
- **Scorecard:** correctly flags **apples k3 saturation ~1.49 = oversupplied crowded lake**
  (its stated failure mode); correctly flags **samosas k143 sat 0.267, demand 0.866, rising** =
  volume king; correctly reads aerospace finals as thin (no price, sat >1.2). **Cannot see
  crude/dough/engines at all** → must not be the sole gate or it silently kills every B2B good.

### S4. `amountSoldRestaurant` (board-data `cmo.retail[].retailData[].amountSoldRestaurant`)
- **What it is:** the Catering/restaurant B2B off-take volume, units/day — a *realized* B2B
  turnover number that S1/S2 miss. Latest-day values are large and discriminating: milk
  11,084,582; coffee-ground 2,901,036; **samosas 376,539** (matches the known ~380k/day);
  orange-juice 5,228,836; apple-cider 4,774,653; butter 663,424; salad 306,159; hamburger
  34,505; lasagna 22,362.
- **Reliability: HIGH for the goods it covers** — it is measured off-take, not a static cap, and
  it is the cleanest confirmation of the samosa volume-king truth.
- **Coverage boundary:** only finished goods that restaurants buy. Still blind to crude/dough/
  engines. (Dough is consumed by restaurants *as an ingredient of* their output, so it shows up
  here only *indirectly*, via the recipe graph — reinforcing that S8 is unavoidable.)
- **Scorecard:** scores **samosa/Catering HIGH** (the metric-proof case) ✓. Silent on crude/
  dough/aerospace.

### S5. Exchange SELL-book depth + turnover (board-data `coo.orderBooks` / `cmo.orderBooks`)
- **What it is:** SELL-side listings `[{price, quantity, quality}]` = **SUPPLY depth**. Deep book
  = oversupplied lake, the OPPOSITE of demand (as the task states).
- **Reliability: LOW as a demand signal, and near-useless this cycle.** In the current
  board-data the books cover **only 7 kinds (2,3,4,5,66,111,119)** — the goods the bot itself
  currently trades, not a realm-wide supply map. board-data is flagged possibly-stale/empty this
  cycle, and the books confirm it: no crude/dough/aerospace present.
- **Correct use:** as a *penalty/denominator* (supply glut), never as demand. A shallow book on a
  good with high derived demand = pricing power; a deep book = the apples-style suppressed lake.
- **Scorecard:** would (mis)read a deep water/apple book as "activity"; provides nothing on the
  hard cases. Keep only as a saturation cross-check, weight low.

### S6. Price elasticity / our own sell-rate feedback (knowledge.json `sellRates`, learn.js)
- **What it is:** measured `qty/hours` the game quotes when we place a sell order — a real
  absorption rate that already bakes in store level, weather, saturation. Also `retailAverages`
  (fresh, from the live retail feed) and the price-move-per-unit-we-sell elasticity.
- **Reliability: HIGHEST where it exists, but sparse.** Only 4 goods currently measured
  (k3 apples 524/h reference, k4 345/h reference, k5 136/h fresh, k119 coffee-ground 133/h
  fresh). It is ground-truth realized velocity for *our* SKUs, degrades past the freshness
  policy (retailAverage 60 min, sellRate 360 min), and was partly measured under the now-expired
  ×3 acceleration (`regimeChanged: true`) — those rows are demoted, correctly.
- **Scorecard:** authoritative for the handful of goods we actively sell; **says nothing about
  goods we have never produced (crude, dough, aerospace)** so it cannot classify the hard cases.
  Best used to *calibrate* S3's saturation→velocity mapping, not as the ranker.

### S7. Seasonality (`retailSeason`, `productionSeason` in game-facts)
- **What it is:** flags for goods whose demand/production is seasonal; off-season quotes are
  scarcity prices low-level companies can't realize.
- **Reliability: HIGH as an *exclusion* filter, not a demand meter.** Already handled correctly
  by industry-report.js (it skips seasonal goods). Related realm-event context lives in
  `market.modifiers.resourceProductionModifiers` (e.g. current speed modifiers on kinds
  118/9/142/131/27/108) — these move **supply**, so they shift saturation, not demand; use them
  to explain a saturation swing, never as demand.
- **Scorecard:** none of the seven known-truth cases are seasonal; keep as a guard rail.

### S8. Recipe-graph derived-demand propagation (game-facts `recipe`) — THE RESOLVER
- **What it is:** the reverse recipe graph. For each good, `derivedThroughput(k) = leafSink(k) +
  Σ over downstream consumers c of derivedThroughput(c) × recipeCoeff(c,k)`. This is the only
  construct in the data that assigns demand to a B2B intermediate, by pulling the realized demand
  of everything it ultimately feeds back up through the recipe coefficients.
- **Reliability: HIGH — it is the single signal that classifies every hard case correctly**,
  *provided the leaf sink is `consumption` (S1), not `unitsSoldAnHour` (S2).* Using S2 as the
  leaf re-injects the placeholder-"1" mirage and inflates aerospace (see resolution below).
- **Scorecard: correct on all seven** (see matrix). It is the backbone realizable.js must use.

### S9. Ticker price direction (`board-data.market.ticker[].is_up`) — weak context
- 142 kinds, 52 up / 90 down this cycle. **Reliability: LOW** — a noisy 1-bit momentum proxy.
  Keep only as a tie-breaker; it cannot distinguish demand from a supply shock.

## THE CRUX: what distinguishes crude-oil (real) from aerospace (dead)?

Both read **`consumption` = 0, `unitsSoldAnHour` = 0**, both are absent from the retail turnover
snapshot (S3) and the restaurant feed (S4), both sit on the exchange ticker (S5/S9). **On every
direct field they are identical.** The task's proven failure — consumption + unitsSold cannot
separate them — is confirmed: both are 0/0.

**The only separator is S8 (recipe-graph propagation), and it separates them cleanly.**
Their downstream chains terminate at completely different realized sinks:

| Chain | Terminates in | Real leaf demand (consumption/h) |
|---|---|---|
| crude-oil → petrol, diesel, plastic, carbon-fiber | fuels + universal structural/plastic feed | petrol 19.45 + diesel 19.54 (plus retail 103/105), plastic→construction, carbon-fiber→everything |
| aerospace (cockpit/attitude-ctrl/engines/flight-computer) → starship, satellite, jumbojet, private-jet | near-nobody buys them | satellite 0.0115, single-engine 0.0188, jumbojet 0.0027, private-jet 0.0078, starship/booster 0 |

**Verified propagation (leaf = `consumption` only, the honest fractional sink):**

| resource | kind | derived demand /h | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| crude-oil | 10 | **50.27** | REAL — credit it, B2B |
| planks (constr.) | 108 | 13.85 | REAL high-volume |
| steak | 7 | 1.45 | REAL, low |
| dough | 137 | **1.36** | REAL — credit it, B2B |
| combustion-engine | 52 | 0.985 | thin (cars have low realm draw) |
| samosas | 143 | 0.227 | (its own consumption; volume shows in S4 not here) |
| flight-computer | 80 | **0.0566** | **MIRAGE** ✓ matches known 0.057/h |
| attitude-control | 82 | 0.0366 | **MIRAGE** |
| cockpit | 81 | 0.0335 | **MIRAGE** |
| rocket-engine | 86 | 0.0305 | **MIRAGE** |
| jet-engine | 89 | 0.0264 | **MIRAGE** |

crude-oil (50.27/h) sits **~1000×** above the aerospace cluster (0.03–0.06/h). The flight-computer
result **0.0566/h reproduces the independently-verified realm demand of ~0.057/h to three
digits** — strong evidence the method and leaf choice are correct.

**Why you must use `consumption`, not `unitsSoldAnHour`, as the leaf (verified pitfall):** with
leaf = unitsSold+consumption, the placeholder "1" on satellite/starship/jumbojet, multiplied by
the ×34 rocket-engine coefficient, inflates **rocket-engine to 41.0/h (rank #63)** and the whole
aerospace cluster to 6–41/h — a mirage that looks alive. Switching the leaf to `consumption`-only
collapses rocket-engine 41.0 → 0.0305 while crude only falls 4149 → 50 and stays top-tier. That
one substitution *is* the fix for the paper-$/h bug.

**Rule for realizable.js:**
1. Leaf sink = `consumption` (realm draw), **plus** `unitsSoldAnHour` *only when the good is
   confirmed retail-real by S3* (present in the retail snapshot with a non-null `averagePrice` and
   saturation in a sane band) — this readmits petrol/diesel's real retail 103/105 while rejecting
   satellite's fake "1".
2. Propagate up the reverse recipe graph (S8).
3. Multiply by realized price and divide by a supply-glut penalty from S3 saturation / S5 book
   depth to get realizable $/h. **Never** multiply a static field by a recipe coefficient without
   a real leaf behind it.
4. Cross-check leaves against S4 (restaurant off-take) and S6 (our measured sell-rates).
